# Testing Iter (milestone 1)

**The app:** `build/Iter.app` in this folder. `scripts/run.sh` builds it and opens it, and `scripts/test.sh` runs every test. Iter needs macOS 26 or later; this Mac runs 27.

## Before you start

**Weather is off until you give Iter a forecast source, and that is expected.** Iter has three: Apple Weather (WeatherKit), OpenWeather and Windy. WeatherKit only works for an app signed by a **paid** Apple Developer Program team that has WeatherKit switched on for the app's ID. This Mac signs with a free Personal Team, and Xcode has no Apple ID signed in. OpenWeather and Windy need only an API key, so they work with a free team (see "Turning on forecasts without WeatherKit" below). With none of the three working, Explore, Locations, the trip builder and the spot page each show **one banner at the top** that says why ("Add a weather key in Settings to see light scores.", "Apple Weather isn't enabled for this build. Choose another source in Settings.", or, when the network is down, "Weather is offline. Scores are from the last update at 06:29."), with a **Settings…** button where Settings can help. No row, chip or outlook cell says "No forecast" any more: a row keeps the last score Iter loaded for that spot, or leaves the score slot empty. The real sun and moon times stay exact. That one honest banner is part of what you are testing.

To see scores now, turn on **Debug ▸ Use Sample Weather**. The scores then come from made-up weather, and the screens are labelled "Sample data".

**To turn real weather on** (once you have a paid membership):
1. In Xcode, open **Settings ▸ Accounts**, click **+** and sign in with the Apple ID of the paid Developer Program team.
2. At developer.apple.com, go to **Account ▸ Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles ▸ Identifiers**. Click **+**, choose **App IDs ▸ App**, enter the description "Iter" and the explicit Bundle ID `com.dwjames.iter`.
3. On that App ID, tick **WeatherKit** on the **Capabilities** tab and again on the **App Services** tab, then click **Save**. Allow up to 30 minutes for it to take effect.
4. In `project.yml`, change `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM: VF945J28YU` to your paid team's ID (shown in the top right of the developer site).
5. Run `scripts/run.sh --weatherkit`. This adds the WeatherKit entitlement and lets Xcode create the provisioning profile.
6. Iter ▸ Settings ▸ Weather should now show "Working · last update …" under Apple Weather.

## Turning on forecasts without WeatherKit

Details, limits and licences for each provider are in [docs/DATA-PROVIDERS.md](docs/DATA-PROVIDERS.md).

**OpenWeather** (scores with total cloud only):
1. Sign up at https://home.openweathermap.org/users/sign_up.
2. Subscribe to **One Call by Call** from the One Call 3.0 page or the pricing page. 1,000 calls a day are free. Whether a payment card is needed for that was not confirmed, so read what the sign-up asks for.
3. On the **Billing plan** tab of your account, set the daily limit to 1,000 or below, so you are never charged.
4. Copy your key from https://home.openweathermap.org/api_keys. A new key can take up to 2 hours to activate; "Key rejected" in that time is normal.

**Windy** (scores with low, mid and high cloud):
1. Go to https://api.windy.com/keys and create a **Point Forecast** key.
2. A Testing key is accepted, but Iter will not score from it, because Windy shuffles its data. Settings says "Testing key: Windy's data is shuffled, so Iter won't score from it". Elsewhere the reason reads "Windy's testing key returns shuffled data, so Iter won't score from it." You need a Professional key for real scores.

**Paste the key into Iter.** Open **Iter ▸ Settings ▸ Weather**. Under **Forecast source**, set the picker labelled **Use** to "OpenWeather" (or "Windy"). Optionally set **If it fails, try** to another provider (the default is "None"). In that provider's own section, paste the key into the **API key** field ("Paste your key") and click **Save** (or press Return). Its status line should read "Working · last update 19:40" (your time); until then it reads "Needs an API key" or "Checking…". Once a key is saved the field says "Saved in Keychain. Paste to replace" and a **Remove** button appears. Windy's section also has **Key type** (Testing, Professional) and **Model** ("Best for the spot", "GFS everywhere"). Each key provider shows "Calls today 0 of 800" (400 for Windy) with a stepper for the daily cap, and a **Get a key** link. The key goes to your login Keychain (service "com.dwjames.iter.weather"). It is never written to the repo, to defaults or to a log.

**What to expect.**
* Every screen that shows a forecast or a score names its source ("OpenWeather · updated 19:40"; in lists without the time, for example "Windy · GFS"), as a quiet line. Provider attribution is not on these screens: it is in Settings ▸ Weather (**Data Sources and Attribution**) and Settings ▸ About. A fallback is named too: "OpenWeather (Apple Weather unavailable)". Expanding a window on the spot page lists footnotes for what the provider could not supply.
* OpenWeather gives total cloud only. Reasons talk about cloud cover, and confidence is one step lower for golden hour and blue hour.
* Every place Iter shows (curated, your own, Apple Maps and Ask results, trip stops) gets a forecast for its own coordinate, fetched when it is needed. Forecasts are shared per place per hour and count against the daily cap below.
* OpenWeather has hourly data for 48 hours. Days 3 to 8 are scored from daily summaries and carry low confidence. Beyond the last forecast day Iter still gives a score: it carries the last forecast day's weather forward by whole days ("persistence"), with confidence forced to Low and the note "Beyond the forecast: the last forecast day's weather carried forward." A window that began before the first forecast hour is scored from the first hour. Outlook strips stop at the provider's horizon (8 days with OpenWeather).
* If a later fetch fails, a spot keeps its last good forecast and score; the banner says when the last update was.
* Windy is 3-hourly, interpolated to hours, so confidence drops sooner (24 hours rather than 36 for "high"). Windy has no rain probability; Iter uses the rain amount and says so.
* Iter never makes more than 800 OpenWeather or 400 Windy calls in a UTC day. Settings shows the count. At the cap the status line reads "Daily cap reached (800 of 800)" and screens say "Iter's daily limit for OpenWeather is reached, to stay inside the free allowance. Forecasts resume tomorrow or raise the cap in Settings."

**Development switches.** Instead of Settings you can supply a key for one run with an environment variable (`ITER_OPENWEATHER_KEY`, `ITER_WINDY_KEY`) or a launch argument:

```
open build/Iter.app --args -ITER_OPENWEATHER_KEY <your key>
```

`open` does not pass environment variables to the app, so use the launch argument with `open`. Either one overrides the Keychain, and Settings replaces the key field with a read-only row saying where the key in use came from: "From environment (ITER_OPENWEATHER_KEY)" or "From launch argument".

**When a change takes effect.** Changing anything in Settings ▸ Weather (either picker, key type, model, a cap, saving or removing a key) drops every loaded forecast, and every open screen fetches again with the new setup; saving a key also checks that provider. **Light ▸ Refresh Forecasts (⌘R)** is narrower: it re-requests only the forecasts that failed (no key, key rejected, cap reached, offline), and leaves the ones that loaded alone. The spot page has no Retry button now; ⌘R covers it.

Apple Intelligence (Ask Iter and "Explain") needs Apple Intelligence switched on in **System Settings ▸ Apple Intelligence & Siri**. It is on and working on this Mac.

## A guided tour (about 20 minutes)

1. **Open the app.** Run `scripts/run.sh`, or double-click `build/Iter.app`. You land on **All Trips**. The sidebar has three groups: **Trips** (All Trips, pinned trips, trip folders, then unfiled trips), **Locations** (All Locations and your location folders) and **Find** (Explore). The trip comes first because the plan makes the trip the home of the app. With no trips you see one **New Trip** button with three templates directly under it.
2. **Start from a template.** Click **Canyon Country**. A 4-day trip opens in the builder, with its days and stops on the left and the route on the right.
3. **Read the days.** Under the trip's name, the **overview strip** has one cell per day: the date, the stop count and the day's best window (its symbol, start time and score), with a warning mark on a day that has a conflict. Click a cell: that day is selected, the list scrolls to it and the map highlights it. Each day below is its own box: a header with the day number and date, sunrise and sunset, the stop count and the day's driving, then the day's stops as a timeline. The left column of times reads top to bottom: **Leave** beside a drive, **Set up** beside a stop. A stop shows its session (one control: Sunrise, Sunset, the blue hours or Night), the park time, walk-in and set-up, and the light for that window. With weather off, a banner at the top of the builder says why and the score slot is empty, but every time on the page is still exact.
4. **Watch the drives.** Between two stops of a day, on the timeline, you see the MapKit drive time and distance. The drive from the previous evening's stop opens the next day ("From Horseshoe Bend"). If a drive cannot fit between one stop's light and the next, it turns violet and says how short you are ("Drive doesn't fit: 2 hr, 58 min short"), and the day's header area says "2 conflicts on this day". Between two day boxes, an **Overnight · near Page, AZ** line marks where the night is spent. The route map's switcher ("‹ Day 2 · Thu, Oct 8 ›", with All Days in its menu) matches the strip: the selected day's route and pins are at full strength, the other days are dimmed.
5. **Break the order, then accept the suggestion.** Drag a sunrise stop below a sunset stop on the same day. The day shows its conflicts, and a banner inside that day offers **Reorder by light**. Click **Apply** to accept, or **Dismiss** to ignore it. Iter never reorders on its own.
6. **Undo everything.** Press ⌘Z. Every move, removal, rename, date change and delete can be undone; the Edit menu names the action ("Undo Move Stop"), and ⇧⌘Z redoes it.
7. **Move a stop to another day.** Drag it onto another day's header row or its Add Stop row, or right-click it and choose **Move to Day ▸**. The drive times and schedule recalculate, with a small spinner while MapKit answers.
8. **Change the dates.** Choose **⋯ ▸ Change Dates…** and shorten the trip. Iter warns how many stops will move to the last day before it does anything.
9. **Turn on sample weather.** Choose **Debug ▸ Use Sample Weather**. Scores appear on every stop and the sidebar shows a "Sample data" banner, and the weather banner goes away. A stop shows one event unit (the window's symbol, the score and the time in one band-coloured capsule; hover the symbol for "Sunset") with no band word or bars beside it; the session menu items carry the same symbols.
9a. **What the scores mean.** Click the small **i** at the bottom of the sidebar. A popover shows the five colours with their score ranges, the five light windows with their symbols, how confidence works and the forecast source. Press Esc to close it.
10. **Explore.** Press ⌘2. The first time, macOS asks whether Iter may use your location; allow it to see "Near You · Within 300 mi" first, then "Popular" and a collapsed "More Places". If you decline, the list is one "Spots" list under a banner with **Open Location Settings** (before you answer it offers **Use My Location**). Each row is only light: the spot, its distance after the locality, and one event unit in measured lanes on the right: the window's symbol, the score and the start time together in one band-coloured capsule. The symbol is the spot's **next sunrise or sunset** in the spot's own time: a window counts until it ends, after today's sunset the row shows tomorrow's sunrise, and the tooltip on the start time says "Tomorrow" when it is tomorrow's. Blue hours and night are not in the list; they are in the panel and on the spot page. Symbols: `sunrise.fill` sunrise, `sunset.fill` sunset (`sun.haze.fill` morning blue hour, `moon.haze.fill` evening blue hour, `moon.stars.fill` night); hover the symbol for its window's name. A small spinner replaces the score while that spot's forecast is on its way; rows are fetched in list order as they appear, and "More Places" is fetched when you open it. There is no date control and no "show light for" choice: the list header has "45 places" and one menu with **Near You Radius** (100, 200, 300, 500 mi), Sort By, Category, Known For and Source filters. The toolbar's **Windy** button opens windy.com at the map area you are looking at. Click a row: it selects, and after the double-click interval the list is replaced by that place's light panel. Top to bottom: the header (**‹ Places**, "3 of 16" and up and down steppers); the place (name, locality · distance · origin, and the next event as a large event unit); an image strip running to the column edges; **Good to know** (the best-day card with its score, then the facts and access notes); **Light through the day** (a full-width segmented range control, a readout, the sky band and the cloud and rain plot at the full column width, an hour axis with a time pill at the marker, a legend, then the compass rose with its time scrubber; hover or drag to read any time, and from 350 pt wide a "Drag to read any time" hint); the **outlook** (8 or 10 days; click a day and it opens in place to that day's event units); hour by hour; **Save** and **Add to Trip**; the source line; and **Show Full Page**. The list column is 340 to 520 pt wide and the timeline grows with it. In the list, the arrow keys move the selection and open the panel at once, and Return opens it. In the panel, Up and Down step through the list order (the pin follows), Esc or **‹ Places** goes back to the list at the same scroll position with the row still selected, and ⌘F focuses the search field in both states. Typing or changing the search also returns to the list. Double-click a row, or press **Show Full Page** in the panel, to open the spot page. Right-click a row or pin: Open, Save, Add to Trip, Open in Maps, Copy Coordinates. Selecting a pin opens the panel for it (or switches the open panel to it); clicking empty map clears the selection and returns to the list. Zoomed out, spots that would overlap merge into one numbered capsule with a small dot in the best member's band colour ("● 3"); click it to zoom in until they separate. The selected and hovered spots are never merged. Your location shows as the blue dot on the Explore map and the trip route map (with a locate button) once allowed; none without permission. The map style (Standard, Satellite or Hybrid) is a small map button at the bottom right of the Explore, Locations and trip route maps and under View ▸ Map Style (checkmark on the current one); one choice is shared by every map and remembered, with Standard the default. Selecting a spot (a row or a pin) animates the map to about 25 miles around it (50 miles across the shorter side of the map); if you are already zoomed in closer than that it only pans to centre the spot, and going back to the list leaves the map where it is. Check it by zooming out to the whole region, clicking a spot, then zooming in close and clicking another; Locations does the same. Pan or zoom the map, quit and reopen Iter: the map stays where you left it.
11. **Search Apple Maps.** Press ⌘F, type "Antelope Canyon". Two labelled suggestions appear at the top of the list: **Apple Maps** ("Search Apple Maps for “Antelope Canyon”", marked with a return glyph because a place name ranks it first) and **Ask Iter**. Press Return, or click the Apple Maps row. Apple Maps results appear in their own section, labelled "Apple Maps", and can be saved or added to a trip like any spot. Each is scored from its own coordinate, wherever in the world it is: a spinner shows in the score slot until its forecast arrives, then the next sunrise or sunset and a score. (The map frames the result even after you have moved it by hand; this was gap 11 and is fixed.)
12. **Add your own spot.** Press ⇧⌘N (or the Add Spot toolbar toggle), then click the map. A sheet opens with the pin on a small map, which you can drag to adjust. Iter fills in the place name and time zone. Until the reverse lookup answers, the zone is an estimate from the pin (the nearest curated spot within 150 km, else a whole-hour offset from the longitude), not this Mac's; the lookup replaces it with the real zone when it answers. Pick a category and any best light, then save. The forecast is fetched as soon as you save, so the new spot's score appears in the list within a few seconds.
13. **Open a spot page.** Double-click Mesa Arch. Under the header and the image strip comes **Good to know**, led by the best-day card: the best sunrise in the days the forecast covers, with its range ("Likely 72–100"), then the facts. Below that is **Light through the day**: the timeline (hover to read any hour) and, at its foot, the compass rose with the classic view's direction and the time scrubber. Then the outlook (fainter further out): up to 10 days, but never past the provider's horizon, so 8 days with OpenWeather, and starting tomorrow when today's window has passed; click a day to open it in place to that day's event units, and a window row opens to its reasons (each factor and its effect). There is no intent picker; the page follows the spot's own best light. Then hour-by-hour weather with the source line ("Windy · GFS · updated 09:00"), a **Windy** section with **Open in Windy** (the map opens in your browser, because Windy's terms do not allow its map inside an app like this), and **Look Around** where Apple has imagery.
14. **Ask for an explanation.** On a scored window, click **Explain**. Apple Intelligence writes two or three plain sentences from the listed factors only, and it is labelled as written by Apple Intelligence.
15. **Add to a trip from the spot.** Use **Add to Trip ▸ Canyon Country ▸**. Each day in the menu shows that day's sunset at this spot (the sunset symbol and its score), so the choice of day is a light decision.
16. **Ask Iter.** Press ⌘2, then type "foggy forest within two hours of Portland for sunrise". The **Ask Iter** suggestion ("Ask Iter: foggy forest…") is first, with the return glyph, and **Apple Maps** is second. Press Return, or click the Ask Iter row. A plain place name ("Mesa Arch") ranks Apple Maps first instead (see "Pinning, folders and Ask" below). You see real stages ("Searching near Portland, Oregon…"), elapsed time and **Cancel**. Every result is a real place from Apple Maps or Iter's curated list, in an **Ask Iter** section at the top of the list, each with a one-line note from Apple Intelligence under it. The Light Index (not the model) scores the light.
17. **Locations.** Press ⌘3. Saved curated spots and your own spots are in one list beside their own map, with your own spots tagged "Added by you". Each row shows the spot's next sunrise or sunset (symbol, score, start time) by the same rule as Explore. The map beside it has one event-unit pin per spot, showing the same window as its row; select a row and its pin grows. Right-click to edit or delete your spot (undoable).
18. **Share a trip.** Back in the trip, use **Share** in the toolbar, or **Export…** to save a `.iter` file. Import it with **File ▸ Import Trip…** (⌘O) or by double-clicking the file; it arrives as a new trip.
19. **Settings.** Press ⌘, to set the temperature unit and the default set-up time (there is no default light any more). The Weather tab has the **Use** and **If it fails, try** pickers, then a section each for Apple Weather, OpenWeather and Windy with a status line, a key field (OpenWeather and Windy), the calls used today and a **Get a key** link. The Apple Intelligence tab shows its status.
20. **Light and dark.** Switch the Mac's appearance. Every screen follows it.

## Pinning, folders and Ask

1. **Pin a trip.** Right-click a trip in the sidebar and choose **Pin Trip**. It moves to the top of the Trips group (a pinned trip shows only there, not in its folder) with a pin and a small status glyph. Unpin with **Unpin Trip**. Dragging a trip onto a pinned trip also pins it.
2. **Watch the badge.** The glyph reads "Downloading for offline use, 3 of 9" while it works, then "Ready offline" (hover for the words). The trip's header repeats it with words. A trip shows a warning glyph when it is stale: "Trip changed since download", "Forecast is more than 12 hours old" or "Some items didn't download"; "Couldn't download for offline use" if the pack could not be saved.
3. **What a pinned trip keeps.** On this Mac: the forecast for every stop, the drive times and route lines between stops, the Look Around or satellite image of each stop at the image strip's request width, and the spot details. **The base map is not stored.** MapKit has no public way to download map tiles for offline use, so offline the map may show no detail; the header badge's tooltip says so. Iter refreshes a pinned trip every hour while it runs and when its forecasts are over 3 hours old, through the normal weather path, so the daily call cap applies.
4. **Unpin.** The trip leaves the pinned group at once, but its saved data stays on disk until the next launch, when Iter removes the data of unpinned and deleted trips.
5. **Folders.** Choose **New Folder** from the **+** menu or press ⌥⌘N: the new folder opens with its name selected for typing (Return saves, Esc cancels). Make it in Trips or Locations depending on which screen is open. Right-click a trip, then **Move to Folder ▸** (No Folder or any folder), or **New Folder with Selection** to file it into a new folder. Drag trips onto a folder, drag locations from the Locations list onto a location folder, and drag a folder onto another to nest it (one level only). **Rename** edits the name in the row. **Delete Folder** keeps everything in it and moves it up a level. Every one of these undoes with ⌘Z.
6. **Locations.** **All Locations** lists every saved and own spot beside a map. A location folder shows only its spots (and its subfolders') and a map framed to them; its title is the folder name. Selecting a row selects its pin, and the other way round.
7. **Ask.** In Explore, type a request such as "foggy forest within two hours of Portland for sunrise". There is one field and no Ask toggle. Return always runs the local search: the curated places that match, plus Apple Maps. While text is in the field, the top of the list offers **Apple Maps** ("Search Apple Maps for “text”"), which carries the return glyph. When the text reads like a request, an **Ask Iter** row ("Ask Iter: text", with a sparkle and no header of its own) follows it; place names such as "Great Smoky Mountains National Park" or "Mesa Arch" get no Ask row. Text reads like a request when it has a question mark, starts with a request word or verb ("find", "show me", "where", "best places", "plan", "hike"), or contains as a whole word "near", "within", "for", "with", "sunrise", "sunset", "fog", "forest", "coast", "hours", "drive" or a similar constraint; word count does not matter, and "Hoh Rain Forest" offers the Ask row too. The Apple Maps row goes once its search is running; the Ask row stays beside the results until you run it. Click it to ask. If Apple Intelligence is unavailable, the Ask Iter row stays, disabled, with the reason under it. Results arrive in an **Ask Iter** section with the request as its subtitle and a note under each place; clearing the field removes it. With no place named in the request, Iter uses the area the map shows. **Cancel** stops a running Ask. If Apple Intelligence is off, not ready or unsupported, the section says so plainly and offers **Search Apple Maps Instead**.

## Licence (hidden)

Licensing is built but switched off: this 0.x build is free and nothing is gated (`ITER_LICENSING_ENFORCED` is `NO`). Settings has no Licence tab unless you launch with `-IterShowLicensing YES`; `-IterSettingsTab licence` then opens it. Without the switch nothing about licensing is visible on the Mac or on iOS.

1. With the switch, press ⌘,. A **Licence** tab appears after Updates. It reads "Iter is free while it's in preview" with a **Where's my key?** link, a key field, **This Mac's name** (prefilled) and **Activate**, which stays off until the key is shaped like one.
2. A malformed key keeps **Activate** off. An unknown key, a Mac on the activation limit, being offline and rate limiting each give a short sentence under the form.
3. A licensed Mac shows Status, Licensed to, Activated, the masked key and this Mac's name, with **Check Now** and **Deactivate This Mac…** (which asks first).

It was not exercised against a live store: the tests use a fake server with Lemon Squeezy's documented payloads. Setup and the rules are in docs/LICENSING.md.

## The Debug menu

| Item | What it does |
|---|---|
| Use Sample Weather | Replaces Apple Weather with made-up but plausible weather (all six sky types over any 10 days) so scores can be tested. **Off by default.** While it is on, the sidebar says "Sample data" on every screen, and Settings ▸ Weather says so too. |
| Show Layout Grid | Overlays the 8 pt grid, the 16 pt inset edges and the lane guides on every list and card that calls `.layoutGrid(lanes:)`. Off by default; remembered between launches. |
| Seed Sample Trip | Adds the Canyon Country trip starting tomorrow and opens it. |
| Reset All Data… | Deletes every trip and spot after asking. This cannot be undone. |

For screenshots, launch with `-IterSection explore|locations|trips|trip` (`trip` = first pinned trip, else the most recent; `saved` still opens Locations and `scout` opens Explore) to open on that section (overriding the restored one) and `-IterAppearance light|dark` to force the appearance. Also `-IterSettingsTab general|weather|intelligence|about` to open the Settings window on that tab at launch (`licence` too, with `-IterShowLicensing YES`, which shows the hidden Licence tab), and `-IterSpot <curated spot id>` (for example `-IterSpot mesa-arch`) to open that curated spot's page. `-IterWindowSize WxH` (for example `1280x820`, `960x652`, or `min` for the window minimum) sets the main window's content size at launch, `-IterShowLayoutGrid YES` turns the layout grid overlay on (the same switch as Debug ▸ Show Layout Grid), and `-IterLocation lat,lon` simulates the location behind Explore's "Near you" section. `-IterSelectRow <spot id>` (for example `mesa-arch`) selects that Explore row, scrolls it into view and opens its light panel (`-IterExpandRow` is gone; rows no longer expand), and `-IterPanelScrolled YES` opens the panel already scrolled to its lower half (When to go at the top). `-IterSearch <text>` runs an Apple Maps search in Explore at launch, as if you had typed it and pressed Return. `-IterAddSpot "lat,lon,Name"` adds your own spot there at launch through the same path as the spot editor's Save (it fetches the forecast and selects the spot), and is honoured only together with `-IterInMemoryStore YES`, so it never touches your real data. `-IterSeedLibrary YES` adds a pinned trip, trip folders (one with a subfolder, pinned), location folders (one pinned), saved spots and a pinned location, and `-IterLocationFolder <name>` opens the location folder with that name; `-IterSeedLibrary` works only together with `-IterInMemoryStore YES`. `-IterAsk <text>` runs an Ask with that text in Explore at launch (the search field itself has no Ask switch now). `-IterScoutStub unavailable|results` swaps the Apple Intelligence scout for a stub (`unavailable` reports Apple Intelligence as off; `results` answers with three curated spots near the map and canned notes), and works only together with `-IterInMemoryStore YES`. Also only with `-IterInMemoryStore YES`: `-IterSeedTrip YES` adds the 4-day Canyon Country sample trip (so `-IterSection trip` opens it), and `-IterSeedTrip conflict` adds it with day 2 reversed, which gives that day a conflict and a Reorder by light banner. `-IterTripDay <n>` selects day *n* (1-based) of the opened trip. For performance work: `-IterPerfScript YES` runs a fixed Explore workload (wait for scores, pan and zoom the map, hover pins, select five pins; with `-IterSection trip` it runs the trip script instead: switch days, select stops, nudge a stop, move a stop to another day, then ten seconds idle) and `-IterPerfQuit YES` quits after it; `-IterPerfProbe YES` logs the counters every 5 seconds. Milestones and counters are logged under subsystem `com.dwjames.iter`, category `perf` (`log stream --predicate 'subsystem == "com.dwjames.iter" AND category == "perf"'`), with milliseconds since launch, and as signposts (Points of Interest in Instruments). For example:

```
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection explore -IterLocation 37.77,-122.42 -IterWindowSize 1280x820 -IterSelectRow tunnel-view
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection explore -IterWindowSize min -IterSelectRow mesa-arch -IterPanelScrolled YES -IterAppearance dark
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection explore -IterSearch "Skógafoss"
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection explore -IterInMemoryStore YES -IterAddSpot "64.15,-21.94,Test spot"
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection trip -IterInMemoryStore YES -IterSeedTrip conflict -IterTripDay 2
open build/Iter.app --args -IterInMemoryStore YES -IterSeedLibrary YES -IterSection locations
open build/Iter.app --args -IterInMemoryStore YES -IterScoutStub results -IterSection explore -IterAsk "waterfalls near here for sunrise"
```

To supply a key for one run, see "Development switches" above.

## Known gaps and rough edges

Most important first.

1. **Only OpenWeather has been exercised live.** On 2026-10-06 (about 13:30 UTC) OpenWeather One Call 3.0 was called with the owner's key: the live tests, the app's smoke hook (key read from the Keychain) and the Settings check path (`.working`) all succeeded, and two real responses (`mesa-arch`, `haystack-rock`) are recorded as fixtures beside the documented one. A later live run the same day, after the light display was simplified, searched Explore for a place in Iceland and saw the result listed and scored from OpenWeather for its own coordinate (the camera gap below is from that run). Real responses omit `visibility` in some hours (fog at the coast) and always carry hourly `uvi` and `wind_gust`; the mapping handles both. Windy and WeatherKit have still not seen a real response: WeatherKit needs a paid team (see above), and Windy is tested only against documented-schema fixtures (`Packages/IterKit/Tests/IterServicesTests/Fixtures/README.md`).
2. **Windy's logo is not shipped.** Windy's terms require its logo, unscaled and linked to windy.com, wherever its data appears. Iter shows the required text and a link only, and only in Settings (see the next entry). This must be fixed before any release, and a Windy Testing key cannot be used for scores in any case.
3. **Attribution placement.** At the owner's instruction (2026-10-06, "Take off the attribution, put that in settings.") provider attribution (Apple Weather mark and legal link, "Weather data © OpenWeather", Windy's text and link, "Light Index modified from forecast data") appears only in Settings ▸ Weather and Settings ▸ About, not beside the forecasts. The providers' terms as documented ask for it where the data is shown. This is a test-build choice and must be revisited before any release.
4. **Whether OpenWeather's free allowance needs a payment card was not confirmed** from the pages read.
5. **Ask Iter is only as good as Apple Maps text search and the small on-device model.** Every place it returns is real (that is enforced), but it sometimes returns weak matches, such as a town called Forest Grove for "forest", or a suburban park. Its notes are now limited to what the place data says, so they can be bland. A request typically takes 12–25 seconds, occasionally longer.
6. **Not tested by hand:** drag and drop, map clicks (Add Spot), hover on the map, Look Around and Open in Maps were built and compiled but could not be exercised without using the screen. The same goes for the rebuilt trip builder (2026-10-06): dragging stops between day boxes, arrow keys and Delete in the list, the overview strip and the map's day switcher were checked only by model tests and offscreen renders; and in Explore, clicking a cluster of pins to zoom in was checked by model tests only. Please try them first. Folders, dragging trips and locations into folders, pinning a trip, the offline download and its status badge, and the **Ask Iter** section (a real Ask on this Mac's Apple Intelligence) were checked only by model tests, the `-IterSeedLibrary`, `-IterAsk` and `-IterScoutStub` launch hooks and offscreen renders; please try them by hand. Also in Explore: the light panel's clicks (a row opening it after the double-click interval, a pin opening or switching it, empty map closing it), double-click to open the spot page, the arrow keys in the list and Up and Down in the panel, Return, Esc and the panel's keyboard focus, hover and drag on the panel's timeline, the search suggestion rows and Return running the top one, and Back returning to the same scroll position were checked only by model tests and offscreen renders (and the `-IterSelectRow`, `-IterPanelScrolled` and `-IterAsk` launch hooks); they need trying by hand. The image strip's hover chevrons and horizontal paging have not been used by hand either. The location permission prompt, and the map camera being restored after a relaunch, have not been walked through by hand either.
7. **Times follow your Mac's clock format.** On a 24-hour Mac you will see "17:25" rather than "5:25 PM".
8. **Light Index weights are heuristics** ("estimated from forecast data"), and confidence thresholds have no verification study behind them yet. A score past about three days out is shown with a range and low confidence.
9. **Which window a list row shows.** Explore, Locations and Ask rows show the spot's next sunrise or sunset in the spot's own time (`LightEngine.nextEvent`): a window counts until it ends; after today's sunset the row shows tomorrow's sunrise; it looks up to 4 days ahead; where the sun gives neither (polar), it falls back to the first non-night window not yet over. Blue hours and night are never in a list row, so a spot that is best at blue hour or night shows its sunrise or sunset instead. The trip builder is the exception: a stop shows its own session on its assigned day.
10. **No spot photos.** The prototype's Wikipedia photos are gone (they are not licensed for a paid app). The panel's image strip shows Apple's Look Around where it exists and a satellite image (with a dot at the spot) otherwise. A live MapKit run on 2026-10-06 found Look Around for only 4 of the 45 curated spots (`tunnel-view`, `valley-view`, `bixby-bridge`, `golden-gate-battery-spencer`); the other 41 show the satellite image alone. The strip is built so users' own photos can be added later.
11. **Fixed: the map did not always move to a search result.** A finished search now frames its results as a programmatic fit unless they are all visible (`aResultOffScreenIsFramedAfterTheMapWasMovedByHand`).
12. **`-IterAddSpot` uses the estimated time zone.** The launch hook skips the reverse geocode, so a spot it adds keeps the zone estimated from its coordinate (nearest curated spot within 150 km, else a whole-hour offset from the longitude), not the real zone. The editor's own Save shows the same estimate until the lookup answers, and the lookup replaces it.
13. **Not in this milestone** (see `docs/ROADMAP.md`): iCloud sync and shared trips, the base map for offline trips (pinned trips already keep forecasts, drives and images), weather swap, Now mode, Night (red) mode, widgets, the iPhone app and its Live Activity, calendar and GPX export, the paywall.

## Tests

- `scripts/test.sh` runs the package tests (`swift test`: domain, light engine (including `NextEventTests`: the day boundary and time zones, persistence beyond the horizon, the outlook clamp), astronomy against USNO, JPL and Meeus reference values, store and undo, `TimeZoneEstimate`, services, view models, and `ForecastStatusTests` for the weather banner state) and the app's hosted tests (`xcodebuild test`).
- `ITER_LIVE=1` (MapKit and WeatherKit) and `ITER_LIVE_AI=1` (Ask and explainer) run the live checks: `cd Packages/IterKit && ITER_LIVE=1 ITER_LIVE_AI=1 swift test`. With `ITER_LIVE=1` and an OpenWeather or Windy key (environment, launch argument or Keychain), the live provider checks run too (`--filter "Weather live"`); without a key they are skipped. They spend real calls from your daily allowance. `--filter WeatherLiveRecordingTests` scores two curated spots from live OpenWeather data and prints a summary; add `ITER_RECORD_FIXTURES=1` to re-record the live fixtures (it refuses to write a file containing the key). `--filter WeatherLiveViewModelTests` runs the Settings check and a spot page against OpenWeather; it reads the key from `ITER_OPENWEATHER_KEY` only. The provider mapping, cache, budget and router tests use fixtures and fakes and need no key; the OpenWeather mapping tests run over the documented fixture and the two recorded live responses (OpenWeather exercised live on 2026-10-06); the Windy fixtures follow the documented schema and are not recorded responses.
- `scripts/snapshots.sh` renders every screen and state to `Design/snapshots/` in light and dark at two sizes. Nothing appears on screen while it runs.
