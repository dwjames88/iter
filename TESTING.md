# Testing Iter (milestone 1)

**The app:** `build/Iter.app` in this folder. `scripts/run.sh` builds it and opens it, and `scripts/test.sh` runs every test. Iter needs macOS 26 or later; this Mac runs 27.

## Before you start

**Weather is off until you give Iter a forecast source, and that is expected.** Iter has three: Apple Weather (WeatherKit), OpenWeather and Windy. WeatherKit only works for an app signed by a **paid** Apple Developer Program team that has WeatherKit switched on for the app's ID. This Mac signs with "a free Personal Team", which is free, and Xcode has no Apple ID signed in. OpenWeather and Windy need only an API key, so they work with a free team (see "Turning on forecasts without WeatherKit" below). With none of the three working, Explore, Saved, Scout, the trip builder and the spot page each show **one banner at the top** that says why ("Add a weather key in Settings to see light scores.", "Apple Weather isn't enabled for this build. Choose another source in Settings.", or, when the network is down, "Weather is offline. Scores are from the last update at 06:29."), with a **Settings…** button where Settings can help. No row, chip or outlook cell says "No forecast" any more: a row keeps the last score Iter loaded for that spot, or leaves the score slot empty. The real sun and moon times stay exact. That one honest banner is part of what you are testing.

To see scores now, turn on **Debug ▸ Use Sample Weather**. The scores then come from made-up weather, and the screens are labelled "Sample data".

**To turn real weather on** (once you have a paid membership):
1. In Xcode, open **Settings ▸ Accounts**, click **+** and sign in with the Apple ID of the paid Developer Program team.
2. At developer.apple.com, go to **Account ▸ Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles ▸ Identifiers**. Click **+**, choose **App IDs ▸ App**, enter the description "Iter" and the explicit Bundle ID `com.dwjames.iter`.
3. On that App ID, tick **WeatherKit** on the **Capabilities** tab and again on the **App Services** tab, then click **Save**. Allow up to 30 minutes for it to take effect.
4. In `project.yml`, change `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM: VF945J28YU` to your paid team's ID (shown in the top right of the developer site).
5. Run `scripts/run.sh --weatherkit`. This adds the WeatherKit entitlement and lets Xcode create the provisioning profile.
6. Iter ▸ Settings ▸ Weather should now show "Working · last update …" under Apple Weather.

## Turning on forecasts without WeatherKit

Details, limits and licences for each provider are in [docs/DATA-PROVIDERS.md](docs/DATA-PROVIDERS.md).

**OpenWeather** (scores with total cloud only):
1. Sign up at https://home.openweathermap.org/users/sign_up.
2. Subscribe to **One Call by Call** from the One Call 3.0 page or the pricing page. 1,000 calls a day are free. Whether a payment card is needed for that was not confirmed, so read what the sign-up asks for.
3. On the **Billing plan** tab of your account, set the daily limit to 1,000 or below, so you are never charged.
4. Copy your key from https://home.openweathermap.org/api_keys. A new key can take up to 2 hours to activate; "Key rejected" in that time is normal.

**Windy** (scores with low, mid and high cloud):
1. Go to https://api.windy.com/keys and create a **Point Forecast** key.
2. A Testing key is accepted, but Iter will not score from it, because Windy shuffles its data. Settings says "Testing key: Windy's data is shuffled, so Iter won't score from it". Elsewhere the reason reads "Windy's testing key returns shuffled data, so Iter won't score from it." You need a Professional key for real scores.

**Paste the key into Iter.** Open **Iter ▸ Settings ▸ Weather**. Under **Forecast source**, set the picker labelled **Use** to "OpenWeather" (or "Windy"). Optionally set **If it fails, try** to another provider (the default is "None"). In that provider's own section, paste the key into the **API key** field ("Paste your key") and click **Save** (or press Return). Its status line should read "Working · last update 19:40" (your time); until then it reads "Needs an API key" or "Checking…". Once a key is saved the field says "Saved in Keychain. Paste to replace" and a **Remove** button appears. Windy's section also has **Key type** (Testing, Professional) and **Model** ("Best for the spot", "GFS everywhere"). Each key provider shows "Calls today 0 of 800" (400 for Windy) with a stepper for the daily cap, and a **Get a key** link. The key goes to your login Keychain (service "com.dwjames.iter.weather"). It is never written to the repo, to defaults or to a log.

**What to expect.**
* Every screen that shows a forecast or a score names its source ("OpenWeather · updated 19:40"; in lists without the time, for example "Windy · GFS"), as a quiet line. Provider attribution is not on these screens: it is in Settings ▸ Weather (**Data Sources and Attribution**) and Settings ▸ About. A fallback is named too: "OpenWeather (Apple Weather unavailable)". Expanding a window on the spot page lists footnotes for what the provider could not supply.
* OpenWeather gives total cloud only. Reasons talk about cloud cover, and confidence is one step lower for golden hour and blue hour.
* Every place Iter shows (curated, your own, Apple Maps and Scout results, trip stops) gets a forecast for its own coordinate, fetched when it is needed. Forecasts are shared per place per hour and count against the daily cap below.
* OpenWeather has hourly data for 48 hours. Days 3 to 8 are scored from daily summaries and carry low confidence. Beyond the last forecast day Iter still gives a score: it carries the last forecast day's weather forward by whole days ("persistence"), with confidence forced to Low and the note "Beyond the forecast: the last forecast day's weather carried forward." A window that began before the first forecast hour is scored from the first hour. Outlook strips stop at the provider's horizon (8 days with OpenWeather).
* If a later fetch fails, a spot keeps its last good forecast and score; the banner says when the last update was.
* Windy is 3-hourly, interpolated to hours, so confidence drops sooner (24 hours rather than 36 for "high"). Windy has no rain probability; Iter uses the rain amount and says so.
* Iter never makes more than 800 OpenWeather or 400 Windy calls in a UTC day. Settings shows the count. At the cap the status line reads "Daily cap reached (800 of 800)" and screens say "Iter's daily limit for OpenWeather is reached, to stay inside the free allowance. Forecasts resume tomorrow or raise the cap in Settings."

**Development switches.** Instead of Settings you can supply a key for one run with an environment variable (`ITER_OPENWEATHER_KEY`, `ITER_WINDY_KEY`) or a launch argument:

```
open build/Iter.app --args -ITER_OPENWEATHER_KEY <your key>
```

`open` does not pass environment variables to the app, so use the launch argument with `open`. Either one overrides the Keychain, and Settings replaces the key field with a read-only row saying where the key in use came from: "From environment (ITER_OPENWEATHER_KEY)" or "From launch argument".

**When a change takes effect.** Changing anything in Settings ▸ Weather (either picker, key type, model, a cap, saving or removing a key) drops every loaded forecast, and every open screen fetches again with the new setup; saving a key also checks that provider. **Light ▸ Refresh Forecasts (⌘R)** is narrower: it re-requests only the forecasts that failed (no key, key rejected, cap reached, offline), and leaves the ones that loaded alone. The spot page has no Retry button now; ⌘R covers it.

Apple Intelligence (the Scout and "Explain") needs Apple Intelligence switched on in **System Settings ▸ Apple Intelligence & Siri**. It is on and working on this Mac.

## A guided tour (about 20 minutes)

1. **Open the app.** Run `scripts/run.sh`, or double-click `build/Iter.app`. You land on **All Trips**. The trip comes first because the plan makes the trip the home of the app. With no trips you see one **New Trip** button with three templates directly under it.
2. **Start from a template.** Click **Canyon Country**. A 4-day trip opens in the builder, with its days and stops on the left and the route on the right.
3. **Read a stop.** Each stop's headline is a backward schedule: "Leave 05:54 · park 06:50 · set up by 07:00". Under it you'll find the session (one control: Sunrise, Sunset, the blue hours or Night), the walk-in time ("walk-in unknown" where Iter doesn't know it), the set-up time, and the light for that window. With weather off, a banner at the top of the builder says why and the score slot is empty (there is no "No forecast" text on a stop), but every time on the page is still exact.
4. **Watch the connectors.** Between two stops you see the MapKit drive time and distance. If a drive cannot fit between one stop's light and the next, the connector turns violet and says how short you are ("Drive doesn't fit: 2 hr, 58 min short"). An **Overnight** divider separates the days.
5. **Break the order, then accept the suggestion.** Drag a sunrise stop below a sunset stop on the same day. The row warns "Out of order", and a banner offers **Reorder by light**. Click **Apply** to accept, or **Dismiss** to ignore it. Iter never reorders on its own.
6. **Undo everything.** Press ⌘Z. Every move, removal, rename, date change and delete can be undone; the Edit menu names the action ("Undo Move Stop"), and ⇧⌘Z redoes it.
7. **Move a stop to another day.** Drag it onto another day's header, or right-click it and choose **Move to Day ▸**. The drive times and schedule recalculate, with a small spinner while MapKit answers.
8. **Change the dates.** Choose **⋯ ▸ Change Dates…** and shorten the trip. Iter warns how many stops will move to the last day before it does anything.
9. **Turn on sample weather.** Choose **Debug ▸ Use Sample Weather**. Scores appear on every stop and the sidebar shows a "Sample data" banner, and the weather banner goes away. A stop's score sits beside its window's symbol (the sunset symbol, hover for "Sunset") and shows its confidence bars; the session menu items carry the same symbols.
10. **Explore.** Press ⌘2. The first time, macOS asks whether Iter may use your location; allow it to see "Near You · Within 300 mi" first, then "Popular" and a collapsed "More Places". If you decline, the list is one "Spots" list under a banner with **Open Location Settings** (before you answer it offers **Use My Location**). Each row is only light: the spot, its distance after the locality, and three fixed lanes on the right: a window symbol, the score chip and the start time. The symbol is the spot's **next sunrise or sunset** in the spot's own time: a window counts until it ends, after today's sunset the row shows tomorrow's sunrise, and the tooltip on the start time says "Tomorrow" when it is tomorrow's. Blue hours and night are not in the list; they are on the card and the spot page. Symbols: `sunrise.fill` sunrise, `sunset.fill` sunset (the outline `sunrise` and `sunset` are the blue hours, `moon.stars.fill` night); hover one for its name. A small spinner replaces the score while that spot's forecast is on its way; rows are fetched in list order as they appear, and "More Places" is fetched when you open it. There is no date control and no "show light for" choice: the list header has "45 places" and one menu with **Near You Radius** (100, 200, 300, 500 mi), Sort By, Category, Known For and Source filters. The toolbar's **Windy** button opens windy.com at the map area you are looking at. Click a row: it selects, and the place card opens over the map (image strip, the next-event summary, When to go, the day's windows, "Coming Up" with today's remaining windows and tomorrow's, the light timeline, sun and moon, hour by hour, Good to know with the access notes, then **Save**, **Add to Trip**, the source line and **Show Full Page**). The arrow keys move the selection. Double-click a row, or press Return, to open the spot page. Right-click a row or pin: Open, Save, Add to Trip, Open in Maps, Copy Coordinates. Selecting a pin selects its row and opens the same card. Your location shows as the blue dot on the Explore map and the trip route map (with a locate button) once allowed; none without permission. Pan or zoom the map, quit and reopen Iter: the map stays where you left it.
11. **Search Apple Maps.** Press ⌘F, type "Antelope Canyon" and press Return. Apple Maps results appear in their own section, labelled "Apple Maps", and can be saved or added to a trip like any spot. Each is scored from its own coordinate, wherever in the world it is: a spinner shows in the score slot until its forecast arrives, then the next sunrise or sunset and a score. (The map does not always move to the result; see the known gaps.)
12. **Add your own spot.** Press ⇧⌘N (or the Add Spot toolbar toggle), then click the map. A sheet opens with the pin on a small map, which you can drag to adjust. Iter fills in the place name and time zone. Until the reverse lookup answers, the zone is an estimate from the pin (the nearest curated spot within 150 km, else a whole-hour offset from the longitude), not this Mac's; the lookup replaces it with the real zone when it answers. Pick a category and any best light, then save. The forecast is fetched as soon as you save, so the new spot's score appears in the list within a few seconds.
13. **Open a spot page.** Double-click Mesa Arch. The page leads with **When to go**: the best sunrise in the days the forecast covers, with its confidence and range ("Likely 72–100"). Below that is the outlook (fainter further out): up to 10 days, but never past the provider's horizon, so 8 days with OpenWeather, and starting tomorrow when today's window has passed. There is no intent picker; the page follows the spot's own best light. Below that are the day's windows, named in words (on today, the windows still ahead plus tomorrow's; a row from tomorrow opens that day), with their reasons (open a scored row to see each factor and its effect), the light timeline (hover to read any hour), the sun and moon arc with the classic view's direction, hour-by-hour weather with the source line ("Windy · GFS · updated 09:00") a **Windy** section with **Open in Windy** (the map opens in your browser, because Windy's terms do not allow its map inside an app like this), and **Look Around** where Apple has imagery.
14. **Ask for an explanation.** On a scored window, click **Explain**. Apple Intelligence writes two or three plain sentences from the listed factors only, and it is labelled as written by Apple Intelligence.
15. **Add to a trip from the spot.** Use **Add to Trip ▸ Canyon Country ▸**. Each day in the menu shows that day's sunset at this spot (the sunset symbol and its score), so the choice of day is a light decision.
16. **Scout with Apple Intelligence.** Press ⌘4 and try "Waterfalls near Portland, Oregon". You see real stages ("Searching near Portland, Oregon…"), elapsed time and **Cancel**. Every result is a real place from Apple Maps or Iter's curated list. The model's reason is labelled "Scout's note", and the Light Index (not the model) scores the light.
17. **Saved.** Press ⌘3. Saved curated spots and your own spots are in one list, with your own spots tagged "Added by you". Each row shows the spot's next sunrise or sunset (symbol, score, start time) by the same rule as Explore. Right-click to edit or delete your spot (undoable).
18. **Share a trip.** Back in the trip, use **Share** in the toolbar, or **Export…** to save a `.iter` file. Import it with **File ▸ Import Trip…** (⌘O) or by double-clicking the file; it arrives as a new trip.
19. **Settings.** Press ⌘, to set the temperature unit and the default set-up time (there is no default light any more). The Weather tab has the **Use** and **If it fails, try** pickers, then a section each for Apple Weather, OpenWeather and Windy with a status line, a key field (OpenWeather and Windy), the calls used today and a **Get a key** link. The Apple Intelligence tab shows its status.
20. **Light and dark.** Switch the Mac's appearance. Every screen follows it.

## The Debug menu

| Item | What it does |
|---|---|
| Use Sample Weather | Replaces Apple Weather with made-up but plausible weather (all six sky types over any 10 days) so scores can be tested. **Off by default.** While it is on, the sidebar says "Sample data" on every screen, and Settings ▸ Weather says so too. |
| Seed Sample Trip | Adds the Canyon Country trip starting tomorrow and opens it. |
| Reset All Data… | Deletes every trip and spot after asking. This cannot be undone. |

For screenshots, launch with `-IterSection explore|saved|scout|trips|trip` (`trip` = first trip) to open on that section (overriding the restored one) and `-IterAppearance light|dark` to force the appearance. Also `-IterSettingsTab general|weather|intelligence|about` to open the Settings window on that tab at launch, and `-IterSpot <curated spot id>` (for example `-IterSpot mesa-arch`) to open that curated spot's page. `-IterWindowSize WxH` (for example `1280x820`, `960x652`, or `min` for the window minimum) sets the main window's content size at launch, and `-IterLocation lat,lon` simulates the location behind Explore's "Near you" section. `-IterSelectRow <spot id>` (for example `mesa-arch`) selects that Explore row, scrolls it into view and so shows its place card on the map (`-IterExpandRow` is gone; rows no longer expand), and `-IterCardScrolled YES` opens the place card already scrolled to its lower content (Save, Add to Trip, the source line). `-IterSearch <text>` runs an Apple Maps search in Explore at launch, as if you had typed it and pressed Return. `-IterAddSpot "lat,lon,Name"` adds your own spot there at launch through the same path as the spot editor's Save (it fetches the forecast and selects the spot), and is honoured only together with `-IterInMemoryStore YES`, so it never touches your real data. For example:

```
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection explore -IterLocation 37.77,-122.42 -IterWindowSize 1280x820 -IterSelectRow tunnel-view
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection explore -IterWindowSize min -IterSelectRow mesa-arch -IterCardScrolled YES -IterAppearance dark
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection explore -IterSearch "Skógafoss"
open build/Iter.app --args -IterSection explore -IterInMemoryStore YES -IterAddSpot "64.15,-21.94,Test spot"
```

To supply a key for one run, see "Development switches" above.

## Known gaps and rough edges

Most important first.

1. **Only OpenWeather has been exercised live.** On 2026-10-06 (about 13:30 UTC) OpenWeather One Call 3.0 was called with the owner's key: the live tests, the app's smoke hook (key read from the Keychain) and the Settings check path (`.working`) all succeeded, and two real responses (`mesa-arch`, `haystack-rock`) are recorded as fixtures beside the documented one. A later live run the same day, after the light display was simplified, searched Explore for a place in Iceland and saw the result listed and scored from OpenWeather for its own coordinate (the camera gap below is from that run). Real responses omit `visibility` in some hours (fog at the coast) and always carry hourly `uvi` and `wind_gust`; the mapping handles both. Windy and WeatherKit have still not seen a real response: WeatherKit needs a paid team (see above), and Windy is tested only against documented-schema fixtures (`Packages/IterKit/Tests/IterServicesTests/Fixtures/README.md`).
2. **Windy's logo is not shipped.** Windy's terms require its logo, unscaled and linked to windy.com, wherever its data appears. Iter shows the required text and a link only, and only in Settings (see the next entry). This must be fixed before any release, and a Windy Testing key cannot be used for scores in any case.
3. **Attribution placement.** At the owner's instruction (2026-10-06, "Take off the attribution, put that in settings.") provider attribution (Apple Weather mark and legal link, "Weather data © OpenWeather", Windy's text and link, "Light Index modified from forecast data") appears only in Settings ▸ Weather and Settings ▸ About, not beside the forecasts. The providers' terms as documented ask for it where the data is shown. This is a test-build choice and must be revisited before any release.
4. **Whether OpenWeather's free allowance needs a payment card was not confirmed** from the pages read.
5. **The Scout is only as good as Apple Maps text search and the small on-device model.** Every place it returns is real (that is enforced), but it sometimes returns weak matches, such as a town called Forest Grove for "forest", or a suburban park. Its notes are now limited to what the place data says, so they can be bland. A request typically takes 12–25 seconds, occasionally longer.
6. **Not tested by hand:** drag and drop, map clicks (Add Spot), hover on the map, Look Around and Open in Maps were built and compiled but could not be exercised without using the screen. Please try them first. Also in Explore: selecting a row to open its card, Return and double-click to open the spot page, and the arrow-key behaviour were checked only by model tests and the `-IterSelectRow` launch hook, and the image strip's hover chevrons and horizontal paging have not been used by hand. The location permission prompt, and the map camera being restored after a relaunch, have not been walked through by hand either.
7. **Times follow your Mac's clock format.** On a 24-hour Mac you will see "17:25" rather than "5:25 PM".
8. **Light Index weights are heuristics** ("estimated from forecast data"), and confidence thresholds have no verification study behind them yet. A score past about three days out is shown with a range and low confidence.
9. **Which window a list row shows.** Explore, Saved and Scout rows show the spot's next sunrise or sunset in the spot's own time (`LightEngine.nextEvent`): a window counts until it ends; after today's sunset the row shows tomorrow's sunrise; it looks up to 4 days ahead; where the sun gives neither (polar), it falls back to the first non-night window not yet over. Blue hours and night are never in a list row, so a spot that is best at blue hour or night shows its sunrise or sunset instead. The trip builder is the exception: a stop shows its own session on its assigned day.
10. **No spot photos.** The prototype's Wikipedia photos are gone (they are not licensed for a paid app). The place card's image strip shows Apple's Look Around where it exists and a satellite image (with a dot at the spot) otherwise. A live MapKit run on 2026-10-06 found Look Around for only 4 of the 45 curated spots (`tunnel-view`, `valley-view`, `bixby-bridge`, `golden-gate-battery-spencer`); the other 41 show the satellite image alone. The strip is built so users' own photos can be added later.
11. **The map does not always move to a search result.** Observed live on 2026-10-06: a search for a place in Iceland listed and scored the result in Explore (from its own coordinate), but the map stayed on the restored region and the result's pin was off screen. The cause has not been looked into.
12. **`-IterAddSpot` uses the estimated time zone.** The launch hook skips the reverse geocode, so a spot it adds keeps the zone estimated from its coordinate (nearest curated spot within 150 km, else a whole-hour offset from the longitude), not the real zone. The editor's own Save shows the same estimate until the lookup answers, and the lookup replaces it.
13. **Not in this milestone** (see `docs/ROADMAP.md`): iCloud sync and shared trips, offline trip packs, weather swap, Now mode, Night (red) mode, widgets, the iPhone app and its Live Activity, calendar and GPX export, the paywall.

## Tests

- `scripts/test.sh` runs the package tests (`swift test`: domain, light engine (including `NextEventTests`: the day boundary and time zones, persistence beyond the horizon, the outlook clamp), astronomy against USNO, JPL and Meeus reference values, store and undo, `TimeZoneEstimate`, services, view models, and `ForecastStatusTests` for the weather banner state) and the app's hosted tests (`xcodebuild test`).
- `ITER_LIVE=1` (MapKit and WeatherKit) and `ITER_LIVE_AI=1` (Scout and explainer) run the live checks: `cd Packages/IterKit && ITER_LIVE=1 ITER_LIVE_AI=1 swift test`. With `ITER_LIVE=1` and an OpenWeather or Windy key (environment, launch argument or Keychain), the live provider checks run too (`--filter "Weather live"`); without a key they are skipped. They spend real calls from your daily allowance. `--filter WeatherLiveRecordingTests` scores two curated spots from live OpenWeather data and prints a summary; add `ITER_RECORD_FIXTURES=1` to re-record the live fixtures (it refuses to write a file containing the key). `--filter WeatherLiveViewModelTests` runs the Settings check and a spot page against OpenWeather; it reads the key from `ITER_OPENWEATHER_KEY` only. The provider mapping, cache, budget and router tests use fixtures and fakes and need no key; the OpenWeather mapping tests run over the documented fixture and the two recorded live responses (OpenWeather exercised live on 2026-10-06); the Windy fixtures follow the documented schema and are not recorded responses.
- `scripts/snapshots.sh` renders every screen and state to `Design/snapshots/` in light and dark at two sizes. Nothing appears on screen while it runs.
